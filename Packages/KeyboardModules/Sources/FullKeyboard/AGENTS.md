# FullKeyboard

iikb's own keyboard, on KeyboardUI and KeyboardStore, and the only target that links `KeyboardArbiter`, the private API.

- `FullKeyboardViewController`: the extension's principal class. Forwards events to the engine, renders, runs ranking jobs.
- Its panels besides `EmojiPanel`:
  - `GifPanel` searches KLIPY (`Klipy`, its key in `Resources/Keys/klipy.key`, kept out of git; one request out at a time, `GifSearch`) and puts a tapped GIF on the clipboard (`GifClipboard`), since a keyboard can only type text, or drags a held one into the app's field.
  - `ClipboardPanel` shows what `ClipboardStore` kept of what you copied, links as one-line badges (`LinkBadge`) in a section ahead of the rest, read when the keyboard appears and when the panel opens, on a queue of its own, never the main thread. iOS refuses that read silently unless Settings › Apps › iikb › Paste from Other Apps is Allow. A copy first seen as the keyboard appears is offered in the bar (`Copied`) with the text that read kept, so the offer reads nothing more; a tap reads it to paste.
  - `TranscriptsPanel` shows what you dictated, from the copy the app writes for it (`TranscriptFeed`), in `ClipboardPanel`'s rows.
  - `SnippetsPanel` is the Snippets panel and the Quicklinks one, in the same rows, read from `SnippetStore` with the settings: a tap types one through the engine (`KeyboardEvent.snippet`, `.quicklink`), holding one offers Copy, a quicklink's Open and Edit (`AppLink.snippets(_:)`), and the snippets' field holds Create Snippet. `FormPanel` is `Panel.form`, a fill's fields: its field is KeyboardUI's `FormField`, a real text field with iOS's caret where Full Access allows focus, as the search fields have. The form always sits above the keys, never in their place. What a fill finishes beyond typing (`TypingEngine.takeFinished`) the view controller does after each event: it keeps a new snippet (with Full Access, the panel asking for it first) or opens a quicklink as other links open.
  - `RewritePanel` is Text Styles and Change Case: each style or case applied to what it would rewrite (`TypingEngine.rewriting`), in `ClipboardPanel`'s rows; a tap sends `KeyboardEvent.rewrite`.
- `MenuView`: the menu in the keys' place (docs/design.md, "The menu"), with cut, copy and paste in the bar (`SuggestionBar.Edit`, through `UIPasteboard` and the engine). Its shelves are KeyboardUI's `MenuShelf`s holding the clipboard, Transcripts, GIF, Text Styles and Change Case panels themselves, laid out as their one-row results; a shelf's name opens its panel.
- `ClipPreview`: what holding a clip, a transcript or a GIF shows, as its context menu's preview.
- `ClipTitle`: how a clip's row, card, link badge and preview write it by its kind: a link as its site's icon (`LinkIcons`) and site, its badge adding how long ago; an address, a number or a color after its glyph.
- `Sources`: the sources you add in the app (docs/sources.md). Each gets the field you type in as JSON, off the main thread, and its hint stands in for the suggestions until tapped. Only with Full Access, never password or code fields.
- `Chats` and `ChatPanel`: the chats of the extensions you add in the app (docs/extensions.md), from the menu's Chats shelf (`ChatShelf`), with Full Access. A chat grows the keyboard once, into the app's room where the rest of the screen can't hold its tabs over a box of photos above the keys or your photos (the folded Duo held sideways), and fills it with the keys down, on iOS's own glass edge to edge as the keys are, the chats as KeyboardUI's `ChatTabs` in the bar (and atop the chat with the keys up); tapping its `MessageField` brings the keys up under it at the same height. Its paperclip brings `ChatPhotos`' grid of your newest photos up in the keys' place instead (`PhotoGrid`, PhotoKit with the access the app asked for, which the keyboard shares; iOS's own picker stays on "Loading…" inside a keyboard): each photo tapped is attached in the box, made a JPEG of at most 2048 pixels without being decoded whole, and kept beside the unsent messages (`UnsentStore.keep`) until its message is sent. Paste in the box attaches copied photos the same way. The messages are KeyboardUI's `ChatThread`, real text you hold to select and copy, whose links open through the host app. While it's open the engine types into its `LineDocument` in place of the app's field, so suggestions, autocorrect and the mic work there; return starts a new line, as in Messages, and the box's ↑ finishes the word (`finishWord`) and sends all of it (`take`) to the open chat, with its photos or as photos alone.
- A panel opened from the menu (Clipboard, Transcripts, GIFs) fills the keys' place until you tap its field, which brings the keys up with it above them; back steps out a level at a time, to the menu. The emoji keyboard, from the emoji key, does the same with its field on top, as Apple's does: its emoji key and return lower the keys again to its pages, and ABC closes it. The open chat's messages are asked for again as each answer comes (the extension holds the request until they change), only while the panel is open. A read that fails stops the asking until the network changes, you send, you touch the chat or the keyboard appears, never on a clock, so staying reachable through its own restarts is the extension's job (docs/extensions.md, "What iikb asks"). Switching back to a chat shows its last answer at once, and the list and each chat's last 40 messages are kept on disk as the keyboard goes away, so a new keyboard shows them at once while it asks again. Sources aren't told what you write there. A chat open as the keyboard goes away opens again in the next keyboard in the same field, with its line, keys and drafts, so dictating into it can go through the app and back. iOS takes the field from a keyboard it's about to end (the iPad, back in an app), which isn't a move to another field, so the chat stays open and that keyboard keeps it for the next. After Allow Photos, until the photos are allowed, it opens in any field of the app it was asked from, since iOS names some fields anew on coming back (Safari's, Contacts'), and waits through other apps' fields, iikb's own included. The App Store build can't tell apps apart, so there it waits for the same field. It's kept on disk (`KeptChat`), not in memory: iOS ends the keyboard's process while the app is in front, and at its memory limit, 177 MB on the Duo (its JetsamEvent reports, Nov 9).
- `FieldLines`: the space bar trackpad's up and down by the lines the field really wraps, as Apple's keyboard moves. Nothing a keyboard is given says where a field wraps (KeyboardCore/AGENTS.md, `Sentences`), but the app's accessibility tree does: AXRuntime's `AXElement` (private) names the focused field, its text, its selection and the rectangle of any character, so a move finds the line above or below and the character nearest the caret's x on it. It trusts a field only when its text before the caret ends as the keyboard's does, works off the main thread, and moves through the engine's `.cursor`. iOS doesn't let the keyboard reach the app's accessibility on the device, only in the simulator, so there up and down move by the lines of a measured field (`MeasuredField`, `Resources/fields.json`, docs/trackpad.md), else by sentence. Each app's answer, its lines or where asking stopped, is kept in Application Support's `field-access.json`, which reaches the backup. The App Store build leaves it out.
- `KeyClicks`: each key's sound as Apple's keyboard plays it, while Keyboard Feedback › Sound is on in iOS Settings, read from iOS's sound settings (`com.apple.preferences.sounds`, `keyboard-audio`) as the keyboard appears, the same read UIKit's own input click makes in a keyboard extension, so iikb's sounds follow the setting exactly as far as UIKit's click would.
- The typing log (scripts/AGENTS.md, "scripts/replay"): with Settings › Privacy & Data › Typing Log on and Full Access, the view controller gives its engine a `SessionLog` as it loads, before the engine meets a field, so the log starts from a fresh engine, and saves it through `SessionLogs` as the keyboard goes away and as a message goes. A password, one-time code or card field (`isPrivate`) ends the log at once, kept as it was before the field, for the rest of that keyboard; turning the setting off drops it unsaved.
- `KeyboardHost`: the app the keyboard is in, from the moment it appears (docs/sources.md, "Which app you're typing in").

## Opening apps and knowing the host

- Apps can't be opened from a keyboard through public API; iikb walks the responder chain to the host's `UIApplication`, as Wispr Flow and others do.
- Since iOS 26.4 a keyboard's own properties don't name the app you were typing in, and a keyboard can't reopen it. So `KeyboardHost` matches its host's process ID to the app UIKit's keyboard arbiter names, which the C target `KeyboardArbiter` switches on as the keyboard loads, and names it in the dictation link (App/Voice/AGENTS.md). None of it is API, so the App Store build (`APP_STORE`, ci_scripts/) leaves it out: there the host is unknown, nothing below gets it, and voice typing leaves you in iikb to swipe back.

Host identity is keyboard-wide context, not voice typing's alone: `KeyboardHost` resolves it on appearance and UIKit text/selection events, and whatever gains from knowing the app takes it from there.
- Sources receive its bundle ID (`app`, empty when unknown).
- A chat kept after Allow Photos waits for its host's process ID (`KeyboardHost.process(of:)`), not its name: a keyboard iOS has just started holds the ID from the start, but the arbiter can name the app only later, and never iikb's own.
- Language ranking learns per-app word and next-word counts over shared vocabulary.
- Emoji choices and GIF recents prefer this app's picks.
- Typing stats count each hour under its app.
- Dictation is written in the Writing Style picked for it, else the default (`KeyboardSettings.dictation(in:)`): the app styles it for the transcript's app, and the engine sets its capitals for `TypingEngine.app`.
- Voice typing returns to it, and leaves for the app only when it is an app iOS can reopen, never a share sheet or another host leaving would close (`KeyboardHost.returns(to:)`, LaunchServices), and each transcript keeps the app it was typed into (`Dictation.host`), for Transcripts and Stats › Dictation.
- With the microphone off, a tap on the one mic records in the keyboard itself (`LocalDictation`, which carries UIKit's and WebKit's events to KeyboardCore's `KeyboardDictation` and does what it says): iOS won't record in a keyboard extension, so a hidden `WKWebView` in it captures through `getUserMedia`, in WebKit's media process under the microphone permission the app was given (the keyboard's own reading, or the app's, `Dictation.micAllowed`, since a keyboard keeps the answer it read as it started), into a `Recording` in the keyboard's temporary folder. The web view is one for the keyboard's process, made and its page loaded without the microphone, so no orange dot shows until you dictate, and moved into whichever keyboard shows; a keyboard going away takes it out of its own view only, never a newer one's. It's made once the keyboard's first frame is drawn (`FirstFrame`, which logs how long the keys took to it), as haptics start then, since iOS drops a keyboard slow to draw (Hex's, on devices): a finger can't land on the mic before then, and one that does before the page loads starts it. Hearing starts as a finger lands on the mic, so the first word isn't lost while WebKit opens it; a finger that leaves without a tap throws it away, and so does one still resting there after 3 s, a backup for a touch-up that never came. Until the page sends its first sound the mic shows a spinner (`MicButton.Phase.starting`), and a tap then undoes the start. It asks for the microphone picked in Settings › Microphone by its name, else, as the app's Automatic does, a wired or USB one the keyboard's own session lists, else the built-in one: never a Bluetooth one unless it's picked. WebKit records in call mode in its own process, which nothing the keyboard sets reaches, so the page asks for echo cancellation: Apple's voice processing, several microphones, noise cut and level set. Without it, as Hex's keyboard records, speech comes in about 40 dB lower (a median of −54 dBFS over 32 dictations, against −13). Rounded to 16 bits in the page and again in the file before Parakeet levels it, quiet speech loses words: voice-processed dictations turned down 40 and 50 dB went from 3.17% of words wrong to 4.35% and 5.39% (`scripts/voicebench`). Call mode pauses what's playing and WebKit never says it may play again, so whether something plays is read before the page starts, and once WebKit lets the microphone go the keyboard's own session goes active and off with `notifyOthersOnDeactivation`, for players that resume on it (unverified on the iPhone). Another sound (a notification, an alarm, a call) takes the audio session from WebKit, which interrupts the page's `AudioContext` and mutes or ends its microphone track and may leave them so: the page tells the keyboard when it's interrupted and back, and as a backup for a resume WebKit never makes, every 250 ms resumes a context that isn't running and opens the microphone again for a track that ended or stayed muted 3 s (again after 1, 2, 4, then every 8 s), into the same recording. A capture that sends no sound for 6 s ends with what it heard (or, never started, says so), but not while it's interrupted: then it waits up to 15 s, a notification passing in about one, a call or an alarm not. Hex's keyboard found a notification ended its dictations this way (TestFlight build 147); what iOS does to the capture on the iPhone for one is unverified here. Voice processing is what the app dropped for recordings with no sound (VoiceCapture/AGENTS.md), so a keyboard dictation of a second or more whose every sample is zero, with no words written, throws its web view away and the next starts on a fresh one (whispers recorded raw peaked under 100/32767 on Nov 25, and two Parakeet wrote right were dropped as no sound), and Transcripts marks keyboard dictations as voice processed so `scripts/voicehealth.py` counts them apart. iOS ends a keyboard past its memory limit (`KeyboardMemory`), so a dictation starts only with 32 MB left (24 once the page is loaded), else the tap opens the app. A memory warning, in this keyboard or an older one still alive, lets Parakeet go (one writing keeps it until that writing ends), which isn't loaded again until a keyboard shows, and lets the web view go unless a dictation records with it. Once you stop, the picked model writes it down there as the app would (VoiceCore: Parakeet v3, which Ultra writes as, or Apple's), in the Writing Style picked for the host app, with your Vocabulary (`VocabularyFeed`), and it's typed as the app's dictations are, in the order said, a new one startable while the last is written. Parakeet is loaded ahead: once you've dictated in the keyboard before, as its web view is readied after the keyboard's first frame and after each dictation is written, never while one records, with 84 MB left (60 for the load, from Hex's measures, and 24 for a dictation started during it), so the 17 to 37 s compile after an install happens while you type. A dictation stopped while it loads waits for it, the bar saying "Model loading…", and one stopped with Parakeet not loaded loads it then, with 60 MB left. With a Parakeet picked, Apple's model never writes a dictation, here as in the app: `KeyboardDictation.writer(picked:seconds:room:)` picks Parakeet or the app, never Apple's (`KeyboardDictationTests`). Parakeet writes only where it fits (`KeyboardDictation.Room`, from Hex's measures: a memory limit of 131 MB or more, and 24 MB with 0.5 MB a second of sound to write), one window at a time, and only on the Neural Engine: one whose encoder lands on the CPU, about 425 MB of weights, is let go and set aside for as long as the keyboard runs. Where iOS counts the Neural Engine's memory against the keyboard (`KeyboardMemory.neural`, iOS 27), the encoder is on it when that grew 100 MB or more over the load (Hex's measure), read without running it, since timing a window through it on the CPU, as the app does, could itself end the keyboard; elsewhere it's timed. A recording stops at 90 s and is written as if you'd tapped stop, the bar saying so: Parakeet holds the whole sound, 0.5 MB a second, so 90 s needs 69 MB of the 86 MB a keyboard with it loaded has left recording under the Duo's 177 MB, and Apple's model, when it's the one picked, is handed the whole sound too. Apple's writes, with your Vocabulary as hints, only when it's picked and only once iOS has its assets (the keyboard never downloads them), and Transcripts names which model wrote. A dictation Parakeet can't write here (no room, set aside, a load or a write that fails, let go by a memory warning) or not written in 2 minutes is left for the app's Parakeet to write, with its sound, and the bar says so; one Parakeet heard no words in is just that. What each capture and writing did, the time from touch to first sound included, goes to iikb's log and to `KeyboardVoiceLog`, which the app adds to its `voice.log`, since it can't read the keyboard's. Each is left for the app with its sound (`Dictation.Made`), written, or for the app to write when you canceled it, so Transcripts keeps every one and the Transcripts panel shows it at once. When the keyboard goes away or its app leaves the front mid-dictation, what was said is still written down, and typed if a keyboard comes back to that field within 30 s (`dictation-held.json`), else kept in Transcripts. Each capture has a number the page sends back, so a stopped one never reaches the next, a start stopped before it got the microphone lets it go, and a page whose WebKit process ended loads again. A sound a keyboard iOS ended mid-dictation left is handed to the app by the next keyboard, as not typed. Only a tap the keyboard can't hear or write (no microphone permission yet, no finished download of Parakeet yet, too little memory, a capture that won't start) opens the app as above. With the microphone on, the mic is the plain button it always was.

Counts and picks stay local; the GIF service receives no host ID. App identity cannot establish clipboard origin or a conversation. A new use goes through `FullKeyboardViewController.hostApp`, never a lookup of its own.
